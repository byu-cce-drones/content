# Flight Checklist Lab

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Flight checklists help reduce human error and ensure important safety and operational steps are not forgotten.
2. Drone operations can generally be organized into **pre-flight, during-flight, and post-flight** procedures.
3. A checklist should be adapted based on the drone, equipment, environment, and type of mission being performed.
4. Pilots should be familiar with their aircraft and supporting equipment before beginning a flight.
5. Consistent procedures help make drone operations safer, more efficient, and more repeatable.

---

## Background

Flying a drone involves much more than simply controlling the aircraft. Before a flight begins, the pilot must make sure the aircraft, equipment, flight area, and mission are ready. During the flight, the pilot must continue monitoring the aircraft and surrounding environment. After landing, the aircraft and collected data must be checked and the equipment properly stored.

One of the most effective ways to organize these responsibilities is through the use of a **flight checklist**.

Flight checklists help pilots remember important steps and reduce the likelihood that something will be overlooked. Even experienced pilots use checklists because routine tasks can be easy to forget, especially when operating different aircraft or performing more complex missions.

Most drone flight procedures can be divided into three general stages:

* **Pre-Flight** – Preparing the aircraft, equipment, flight area, and mission before takeoff.
* **During Flight** – Monitoring the aircraft, environment, and mission while the drone is operating.
* **Post-Flight** – Safely shutting down the aircraft, inspecting equipment, verifying collected data, and preparing the system for storage or future use.

Although many checklist items apply to almost every drone operation, the exact checklist will change depending on the aircraft and mission.

For example, a small training drone may only require a basic aircraft inspection, battery check, controller connection, and flight-area inspection. A professional mapping drone may require additional checks involving GPS, RTK corrections, camera settings, storage media, mission planning, and other supporting equipment.

During this lab, you will examine several of the drone systems and related equipment that will be used throughout the course. The instructor and TAs will demonstrate how the different systems are stored, assembled, prepared, and powered on.

As you examine the different aircraft, pay attention to which procedures are common between systems and which procedures are specific to a particular drone.

This will help you begin developing a general flight procedure that can later be adapted to different aircraft and missions.

## Flight Checklist Structure

A flight checklist helps organize the tasks that should be completed throughout a drone operation. While the exact items may vary, most checklists can be divided into three main stages:

### Pre-Flight

The pre-flight checklist is completed before the aircraft takes off.

This section should help confirm that the aircraft, equipment, environment, and mission are ready for safe operation.

Common pre-flight items may include:

* Inspecting the aircraft for visible damage
* Inspecting propellers
* Checking battery charge levels
* Installing batteries correctly
* Verifying the controller is ready
* Checking the flight area for people and obstacles
* Reviewing weather conditions
* Reviewing airspace requirements
* Reviewing the planned flight or mission
* Verifying the aircraft and controller are connected
* Checking GPS or positioning status, if applicable
* Verifying camera or sensor settings
* Confirming sufficient storage space
* Confirming the takeoff and landing area is clear

!!! note "Before Takeoff"
    Many problems are much easier to correct while the aircraft is still on the ground. A thorough pre-flight checklist can prevent unnecessary risk once the drone is airborne.

---

### During Flight

The during-flight checklist focuses on the items that should continue to be monitored while the aircraft is operating.

Common during-flight items may include:

* Maintaining visual line of sight with the aircraft
* Monitoring battery level
* Monitoring aircraft position and altitude
* Watching for people, vehicles, or obstacles entering the flight area
* Monitoring weather conditions
* Watching for warnings from the controller or flight software
* Confirming that the aircraft is following the intended flight path
* Monitoring mission progress
* Maintaining communication with other members of the flight crew
* Being prepared to pause the mission, return, or land if conditions change

!!! warning "Conditions Can Change"
    A safe flight at takeoff does not guarantee that conditions will remain safe. Pilots should continuously monitor both the aircraft and the surrounding environment.

---

### Post-Flight

The post-flight checklist begins after the aircraft has safely landed.

This section helps make sure the aircraft, equipment, and collected data are properly handled before leaving the flight area.

Common post-flight items may include:

* Confirming the aircraft has landed safely
* Stopping the motors
* Powering down the aircraft
* Powering down the controller and supporting equipment
* Inspecting the aircraft for damage
* Removing and storing batteries
* Checking battery condition
* Verifying that required images or data were collected
* Removing or securing storage media
* Accounting for all equipment
* Recording any problems or unusual behavior
* Preparing equipment for storage or the next flight

## Checklist Differences Between Drone Systems

Not every drone requires the same checklist.

Some checklist items apply to nearly every aircraft, such as checking batteries, inspecting the aircraft, and making sure the flight area is clear.

Other checklist items may only apply to certain drone systems.

For example:

### Small Training Drone

A small training drone may require:

* Aircraft inspection
* Propeller inspection
* Battery check
* Controller connection
* Flight area inspection

### Camera Drone

A camera drone may also require:

* Gimbal cover removal
* SD card check
* Camera settings
* GPS connection
* Home point confirmation
* Return-to-home settings

### Mapping or Survey Drone

A mapping or survey drone may require additional checks such as:

* Mission plan verification
* Flight altitude
* Image overlap
* Camera orientation
* RTK connection
* Base station setup
* Coordinate or positioning settings
* Storage capacity
* Data collection settings

!!! question "Think About the Differences"
    Which checklist items would apply to nearly every drone?

    Which checklist items would only apply to certain aircraft or missions?

## Homework

Complete the following assignment after the lab. You will complete the questions in the Learning Suite quiz associated with this lab. The assignment is due before the next lab session.

### Create a Generic Drone Flight Checklist

Create your own generic drone flight checklist that could be used as a starting point for a variety of drone operations.

You will turn in three separate checklist on the following sections:

1. **Pre-Flight**
2. **During Flight**
3. **Post-Flight**

Your checklist should be written so that it applies to most drone operations rather than one specific aircraft.

### Pre-Flight Checklist

Include the steps that should be completed before the aircraft takes off.

Consider topics such as:

* Aircraft condition
* Batteries
* Equipment
* Flight area
* Weather
* Airspace
* Flight planning
* People and obstacles
* Aircraft and controller connection

### During-Flight Checklist

Include the items that the pilot or flight crew should monitor while the aircraft is operating.

Consider topics such as:

* Aircraft position
* Battery level
* Visual line of sight
* People and obstacles
* Weather changes
* Controller or software warnings
* Mission progress

### Post-Flight Checklist

Include the steps that should be completed after the aircraft lands.

Consider topics such as:

* Safe shutdown
* Aircraft inspection
* Battery removal and storage
* Data verification
* Equipment inventory
* Recording problems or unusual events
* Equipment storage

### Checklist Requirements

Your checklist should:

* Be divided into **Pre-Flight, During Flight, and Post-Flight** sections.
* Be written as a usable checklist rather than a paragraph.
* Be organized in a logical order.
* Include enough detail that another person could reasonably follow it.
* Be written for a generic drone operation.
* Include safety, equipment, environmental, and operational considerations.

!!! tip "Think Beyond One Drone"
    Your checklist does not need to include every possible item for every aircraft.

    Focus on creating a strong general checklist that could later be modified for a specific drone, sensor, or mission.
