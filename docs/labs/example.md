# Drone Operations Checklist Lab

!!! abstract "Key Takeaways"
    - Checklists are used to reduce human error, not just to prepare for takeoff.
    - Drone operations require different types of checklists before, during, and after flight.
    - A good checklist is clear, specific, ordered, and easy to use under field conditions.
    - Generic checklists help standardize safe operations.
    - Drone-specific checklists account for differences in aircraft, batteries, sensors, software, payloads, and flight procedures.

---

## 1. Background

In this course, you will work with drones that are larger and more capable than small toy drones. These aircraft can collect useful engineering data, but they also require careful preparation, communication, operation, and shutdown procedures.

A checklist is a tool used to help pilots and flight crews complete important steps in a consistent order. Checklists are common in aviation, engineering, construction, medicine, and other fields where small mistakes can create safety problems or poor-quality results.

For drone operations, a checklist is not only a **pre-flight** tool. A complete drone operation may require several different types of checklists, including:

* Equipment packing checklist
* Site safety checklist
* Pre-flight checklist
* Takeoff checklist
* During-flight checklist
* Emergency checklist
* Landing checklist
* Post-flight checklist
* Data management checklist
* Drone-specific setup checklist

In this lab, you will learn why checklists are important, observe how a larger class drone is prepared, and create your own checklist for safe drone operations.

!!! warning "Why This Matters"
    Most drone problems are easier to prevent on the ground than to fix in the air. A missing propeller check, low battery, incorrect flight mode, forgotten airspace check, or unclear team communication can quickly become a safety issue.

### Part 2 – Types of Drone Operation Checklists

A complete drone operation includes more than just takeoff. Different checklists may be used at different times during the operation.

For this lab, organize drone checklists into the following categories:

1. **Equipment Checklist**
2. **Site Safety Checklist**
3. **Pre-Flight Checklist**
4. **During-Flight Checklist**
5. **Emergency Checklist**
6. **Post-Flight Checklist**
7. **Data Management Checklist**
8. **Drone-Specific Checklist**

As you go through each category, think about what information or actions should be included.

---

#### Equipment Checklist

An equipment checklist helps make sure the flight crew brings everything needed for the mission.

Common equipment checklist items include:

* Drone
* Controller
* Charged flight batteries
* Charged controller
* Mobile device or controller screen
* Propellers
* Extra propellers
* Landing pad
* Memory card or onboard storage
* Battery charger
* Cables
* Tools
* Safety vest or field equipment
* Notebook or digital flight log

!!! note "Field Tip"
    A drone can be perfectly functional but still unable to fly if one small item is missing, such as a cable, memory card, controller, or charged battery.

---

#### Site Safety Checklist

A site safety checklist helps determine whether the location is safe and legal for flight.

Common site safety checklist items include:

* Confirm the flight location.
* Check airspace.
* Check for temporary flight restrictions.
* Check weather conditions.
* Check wind speed and wind direction.
* Identify takeoff and landing areas.
* Identify emergency landing areas.
* Look for people nearby.
* Look for vehicles nearby.
* Look for trees, buildings, poles, signs, and power lines.
* Identify areas where the drone should not fly.
* Confirm communication between the pilot and visual observer.

!!! warning "Common Mistake"
    Do not only check the drone. The site itself must also be safe for flight.

---

#### Pre-Flight Checklist

A pre-flight checklist is completed before the drone takes off. These checks help confirm that the aircraft, controller, software, batteries, and site are ready.

Common pre-flight items include:

* Inspect drone body for damage.
* Inspect arms or landing gear.
* Inspect motors.
* Inspect propellers.
* Check that propellers are installed correctly.
* Check drone battery level.
* Check controller battery level.
* Check mobile device or screen battery level.
* Confirm controller is connected to the drone.
* Open the correct flight software.
* Confirm GPS signal.
* Confirm compass or IMU status.
* Confirm camera or sensor view.
* Confirm home point location.
* Confirm return-to-home altitude.
* Check for warning messages.
* Confirm takeoff area is clear.
* Brief the flight crew.

!!! success "Pre-Flight Goal"
    Before takeoff, the pilot should be able to answer three questions:

    - **Is the drone ready?**
    - **Is the site safe?** 
    - **Is the team prepared?**

---

#### During-Flight Checklist

A during-flight checklist helps the pilot and visual observer monitor the aircraft and surrounding environment while the drone is in the air.

Common during-flight items include:

* Maintain visual line of sight.
* Monitor battery level.
* Monitor GPS signal.
* Monitor controller connection.
* Watch for people, vehicles, birds, and other aircraft.
* Monitor wind and changing weather.
* Communicate with the visual observer.
* Confirm the drone is responding normally.
* Avoid flying over people who are not part of the operation.
* Maintain safe distance from buildings, trees, power lines, and other obstacles.
* Prepare to land if conditions become unsafe.

!!! warning "Common Mistake"
    Do not focus only on the screen. The pilot or visual observer must continue watching the aircraft and the surrounding airspace.

---

#### Emergency Checklist

An emergency checklist helps the pilot respond quickly if something goes wrong.

Emergency situations may include:

* Low battery warning
* Lost GPS
* Lost connection
* Strong wind
* Nearby aircraft
* People entering the flight area
* Drone drifting unexpectedly
* Obstacle avoidance warning
* Propeller or motor issue
* Return-to-home activation

Common emergency checklist items include:

* Stay calm.
* Announce the issue to the flight team.
* Maintain visual line of sight if possible.
* Stop the mission if needed.
* Move the drone away from people and obstacles.
* Return to home if safe.
* Land immediately if conditions require it.
* Report the issue to the instructor or TA.

!!! question "Think About It"
    Why should emergency checklist items be short and easy to follow?

---

#### Post-Flight Checklist

A post-flight checklist helps make sure the drone is safely shut down, inspected, and packed away.

Common post-flight items include:

* Land safely.
* Stop motors.
* Power down the drone.
* Power down the controller.
* Remove the battery.
* Inspect drone body for damage.
* Inspect propellers for cracks or chips.
* Check motors for dirt or damage.
* Record battery usage.
* Allow batteries to cool before charging.
* Pack equipment carefully.
* Report damage, abnormal behavior, or safety concerns.

!!! note "Field Tip"
    A good post-flight checklist helps prepare the drone for the next flight, not just finish the current one.

---

#### Data Management Checklist

A data management checklist helps make sure the flight data, photos, videos, or mapping images are saved correctly.

Common data management checklist items include:

* Confirm photos or videos were recorded.
* Confirm mapping images were saved.
* Check that the memory card or storage device is present.
* Transfer data to the correct folder.
* Use a clear file naming system.
* Back up important data.
* Record flight notes.
* Record problems that may affect data quality.
* Do not delete files until the data has been checked.

!!! warning "Data Quality"
    A flight can be safe but still unsuccessful if the data is missing, blurry, incomplete, or saved in the wrong location.

### Part 3 – Larger Drone Setup Demonstration

Your instructor or TA will demonstrate how to set up one of the larger class drones.

As you watch the demonstration, take notes on the setup process. Pay attention to the order of operations and the checks that are specific to that drone.

Record notes for each category below.

---

#### Aircraft Inspection

Look for:

* Cracks or damage to the drone body
* Loose arms or landing gear
* Damaged motors
* Dirty or blocked sensors
* Missing parts
* Signs of previous crashes or rough landings

---

#### Propeller Inspection

Look for:

* Cracks
* Chips
* Warping
* Loose propellers
* Incorrect propeller placement
* Propellers that are not fully locked into place

!!! warning "Propeller Safety"
    Propellers can cause serious injury. Never install, remove, or touch propellers while the drone is powered on unless instructed by the instructor or TA.

---

#### Battery Inspection

Look for:

* Proper charge level
* Swelling or puffing
* Cracks or damage
* Dirty or damaged terminals
* Battery fully seated in the drone
* Battery warning messages in the flight software

---

#### Controller and Software Setup

Check:

* Controller battery level
* Mobile device or built-in screen battery level
* Correct drone selected in the flight software
* Connection between controller and drone
* Camera view or sensor feed
* GPS status
* Compass/IMU status
* Home point location
* Return-to-home altitude
* Flight mode
* Any warning messages

---

#### Site Setup

Check:

* Clear takeoff area
* Clear landing area
* People nearby
* Vehicles nearby
* Trees, buildings, power lines, and other obstacles
* Wind direction
* Emergency landing locations
* Communication between pilot and visual observer

### Part 4 – Build a Generic Drone Operations Checklist

Create a **generic drone operations checklist** that could be used for most class drone flights.

Your checklist should include at least the following sections:

1. **Equipment**
2. **Site Safety**
3. **Pre-Flight**
4. **During Flight**
5. **Post-Flight**
6. **Data Management**

Each section should include clear action items. Write the checklist so that another student could follow it without needing you to explain it.

- [ ] Check weather and wind conditions.
- [ ] Identify takeoff and landing area.
- [ ] Identify emergency landing area.
- [ ] Check for people, vehicles, trees, buildings, and power lines.

### Pre-Flight

- [ ] Inspect drone body for damage.
- [ ] Inspect propellers for cracks or chips.
- [ ] Check drone battery level.
- [ ] Check controller battery level.
- [ ] Confirm controller is connected to the drone.
- [ ] Confirm GPS signal.
- [ ] Confirm home point.
- [ ] Confirm return-to-home altitude.
- [ ] Confirm takeoff area is clear.

### During Flight

- [ ] Maintain visual line of sight.
- [ ] Monitor battery level.
- [ ] Monitor GPS and controller connection.
- [ ] Watch for people, vehicles, birds, and aircraft.

### Post-Flight

### Part 5 – Build a Drone-Specific Checklist

A generic checklist is useful, but different drones may require different setup steps. For example, one drone may have removable propellers, another may have folding arms, and another may require a specific controller, app, camera, payload, sensor, or calibration process.

Choose one drone used in class and create a **drone-specific checklist** for that aircraft.

Your checklist should include:

* Drone name or model
* Required equipment
* Battery setup
* Propeller setup
* Controller setup
* Software/app setup
* Sensor or camera checks
* Takeoff procedure
* Landing and shutdown procedure
* Data management steps
* Any special warnings or common mistakes for that drone

---

#### Example Drone-Specific Checklist Structure

```markdown
## Drone-Specific Checklist

### Drone Model

Drone: ___________________________

### Required Equipment

- [ ] Drone
- [ ] Controller
- [ ] Charged drone batteries
- [ ] Charged controller
- [ ] Propellers
- [ ] Mobile device or controller screen
- [ ] Landing pad
- [ ] Data storage card, if needed

### Pre-Flight Setup

- [ ] Unfold or assemble drone correctly.
- [ ] Inspect drone body.
- [ ] Install battery.
- [ ] Install propellers.
- [ ] Power on controller.
- [ ] Power on drone.
- [ ] Open flight software.
- [ ] Confirm drone connection.
- [ ] Check warning messages.
- [ ] Confirm GPS/home point.
- [ ] Confirm camera/sensor view.
- [ ] Confirm return-to-home altitude.

### Takeoff

- [ ] Confirm takeoff area is clear.
- [ ] Announce takeoff to the team.
- [ ] Start motors.
- [ ] Lift off slowly.
- [ ] Hover briefly and confirm stable flight.

### During Flight

- [ ] Maintain visual line of sight.
- [ ] Monitor battery level.
- [ ] Monitor signal strength.
- [ ] Watch for obstacles and other aircraft.
- [ ] Communicate with the visual observer.
- [ ] Return or land if conditions become unsafe.

### Landing and Shutdown

- [ ] Announce landing to the team.
- [ ] Confirm landing area is clear.
- [ ] Land slowly.
- [ ] Stop motors.
- [ ] Power down drone.
- [ ] Power down controller.
- [ ] Remove battery.
- [ ] Inspect drone and propellers.
- [ ] Save data.
- [ ] Pack equipment.
```

### Part 6 – Checklist Review and Improvement

After you create your checklists, review them carefully.

Ask yourself:

1. Are the checklist items clear?
2. Are the checklist items in a logical order?
3. Are any important steps missing?
4. Are any items too vague?
5. Could another student follow the checklist without extra explanation?
6. Does the checklist include safety, equipment, flight, and data concerns?

Revise your checklist to make it more specific and easier to follow.

---

#### Weak Checklist Items

Avoid vague checklist items such as:

* [ ] Check drone.
* [ ] Check site.
* [ ] Make sure everything is good.
* [ ] Fly safely.
* [ ] Save stuff.

---

#### Stronger Checklist Items

Use specific checklist items such as:

* [ ] Inspect each propeller for cracks, chips, or warping.
* [ ] Confirm return-to-home altitude is higher than nearby trees and buildings.
* [ ] Verify the controller is connected to the correct drone.
* [ ] Identify an emergency landing area before takeoff.
* [ ] Confirm photos or mapping images were saved before leaving the site.

!!! success "Checklist Goal"
    A good checklist should be specific enough to be useful, but short enough that the pilot will actually use it.
