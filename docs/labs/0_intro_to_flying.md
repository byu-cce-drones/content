## Key Takeaways

1. Safe drone operation begins before the aircraft ever leaves the ground.
2. Understanding the controller is essential for maintaining control of the aircraft.
3. Small control inputs are generally more effective than sudden or aggressive movements.
4. Takeoff, hovering, orientation, and landing are foundational skills for all drone operations.
5. A pilot should always maintain awareness of the aircraft, surroundings, people, and potential hazards.

---

## Background

Before operating larger and more professional drones, it is important to understand the basic principles of controlling an unmanned aircraft.

Although many modern drones include GPS positioning, obstacle avoidance, automated flight modes, and other assistance systems, pilots must still understand how their control inputs affect the aircraft. A pilot also needs to know how to safely respond when the aircraft does not behave as expected.

In this lab, you will use a Holy Stone Mini Drone to practice basic drone controls in a controlled environment. These small drones allow you to develop basic flight skills before working with larger and more expensive aircraft later in the course.

You will practice:

* Pre-flight safety procedures
* Identifying the primary controls on the drone controller
* Safe takeoff and landing
* Maintaining a stable hover
* Moving the drone forward and backward
* Moving the drone left and right
* Rotating the drone using yaw
* Maintaining awareness of the drone's orientation

!!! warning "Safety First"
    These drones are small, but their propellers move quickly and can still cause injury or damage. Always follow the instructions of the instructor or TAs and stop flying immediately if directed to do so.

## Basic Drone Controls

Before flying, familiarize yourself with the controller.

Drone movement can generally be described using a few basic types of motion:

* Moving up and down (altitude control)
* Rotating left and right
* Moving forward and backward
* Moving side to side

A TA will demonstrate how to safely operate these mini drones before you begin flying. You will have time to practice and become comfortable with the controls under supervision.

!!! note "Controller Layout"
    The exact location of controls depends on the specific controller being used. Your instructor or TA will demonstrate how to operate the drone you are using in this lab.

As you continue through the course, you should also take time to become familiar with any new drone systems you are introduced to, since different aircraft may use different controllers, software, or flight behaviors.

### Safety Procedures

Before each flight, the pilot should evaluate both the aircraft and the surrounding flight area.

#### Before Flight

1. Inspect the drone for visible damage.
2. Check that all propellers are attached and undamaged.
3. Verify that the battery is properly installed.
4. Verify that the controller and drone are communicating correctly.
5. Identify the front and rear of the aircraft.
6. Check the flight area for:
    * People
    * Obstacles
    * Other drones
    * Loose objects
    * Other potential hazards
7. Confirm that the takeoff and landing area is clear.
8. Do not arm or launch the drone until instructed to do so.
9. Review the flight plan and any specific instructions from the instructor.

#### During Flight

* Maintain visual contact with the drone.
* Keep the drone inside the designated flight area.
* Avoid flying directly toward people.
* Keep a safe distance from other drones.
* Use smooth and controlled inputs.
* Do not intentionally crash or contact obstacles.
* If you become disoriented, stop making aggressive control inputs and attempt to stabilize the aircraft.
* Follow all instructions from the instructor and TAs.

#### After Flight

1. Make sure the propellers have completely stopped.
2. Turn off or disconnect the drone as instructed.
3. Inspect the aircraft for damage.
4. Notify an instructor or TA if the drone was damaged or behaved unexpectedly.
5. Return the drone and controller to the appropriate location.

!!! danger "Do Not Grab a Flying Drone"
    **Danger:** Never attempt to catch or grab the drone while its propellers are moving. If control is lost, prioritize the safety of people in the area rather than attempting to save the aircraft.

## Activity Instructions

### Part 1 – Pre-Flight Inspection and Controller Familiarization

Before flying, work with your group to inspect your drone and identify the controls on the controller.

With the drone not flying, identify the controls used for:

* Throttle
* Yaw
* Pitch
* Roll
* Takeoff/landing, if available
* Any other controls identified by your instructor or TA

As a group, review each control and describe what movement you expect it to produce.

!!! question "Think Before You Fly"
    Think about what you want the drone to do and what control input you need to make that happen. If the drone is facing away from you and you move the directional control to the right, which direction should the drone move?

### Part 2 – Takeoff, Landing, and Hovering

Your first flight exercise will focus on safely getting the drone into the air, maintaining a stable hover, and returning it safely to the ground.

1. Place the drone in the designated takeoff area.
2. Position yourself as directed by your instructor or TA.
3. Make sure the flight area is clear.
4. Take off and raise the drone to approximately 3–5 feet above the ground.
5. Attempt to stabilize the drone.
6. Maintain a hover for approximately 5–10 seconds.
7. Slowly descend.
8. Land inside or as close as possible to the designated landing area.

Once you are comfortable with takeoff and landing, continue practicing hovering as part of the same exercise.

Repeat the full sequence several times.

Your goal is not speed.

Focus on:

* Smooth takeoff
* Controlled altitude
* Stable hover
* Slow descent
* Controlled landing

!!! note "Small Inputs"
    New pilots often overcorrect. If the drone begins drifting, use a small control input and then observe how the aircraft responds before making another correction.

### Part 3 – Directional Control and Aircraft Orientation

Next, you will practice controlling the drone using pitch, roll, and yaw. These movements allow you to move the drone forward/backward, left/right, and rotate its heading.

For this section, you will take off only once and remain in the air while completing all movement exercises. Focus on making small, controlled inputs and stabilizing the drone between each movement.

Quickly running through these basic control movements is a useful habit when manually flying an unfamiliar drone. It allows you to gauge the sensitivity of the controls and understand how the aircraft responds before attempting more precise maneuvers.

#### Forward and Backward Movement (Pitch)

Practice controlling the drone using pitch.

1. Take off and establish a stable hover.
2. Move the drone slowly forward several feet.
3. Stop and re-establish a stable hover.
4. Move the drone backward toward the starting location.
5. Stop and stabilize the hover again.

Repeat forward and backward movements as needed while maintaining a single continuous flight.

#### Left and Right Movement (Roll)

Practice controlling the drone using roll.

1. From a stable hover, move several feet to the left.
2. Stop and stabilize the hover.
3. Move several feet to the right.
4. Stop and stabilize the hover.
5. Return toward the original starting position.

Continue practicing left and right movements while maintaining altitude and staying in a single flight.

#### Yaw and Aircraft Orientation

The direction the drone is facing has a significant effect on how the controls appear to the pilot.

Begin with the drone facing away from you.

1. From a stable hover, rotate the drone approximately 90° using yaw.
2. Stop rotating and maintain a hover.
3. Rotate another 90°.
4. Continue until the drone has completed a full rotation.
5. Return the drone to its original orientation.
6. Land.

Pay attention to how the perceived direction of movement changes as the drone rotates.

!!! question "Orientation Challenge"
    When the drone is facing toward you, its left and right are opposite from your perspective.

    Why could this become a problem if a pilot loses track of the aircraft's orientation?

### Part 4 – Basic Flight Pattern

Combine the skills you have practiced by flying between designated points.

Your instructor or TA will identify a simple course similar to the following:

```text
A ---------------- B
|                  |
|                  |
|                  |
D ---------------- C
```

1. Take off near Point A.
2. Fly from A → B.
3. Stop and hover.
4. Fly from B → C.
5. Stop and hover.
6. Fly from C → D.
7. Stop and hover.
8. Return toward Point A.
9. Land in the designated landing area.

The goal is to make controlled movements between each point, not to complete the course as quickly as possible.  For each leg of the course, focus on:

* Maintaining a stable hover
* Making smooth and controlled movements
* Keeping the drone within the designated flight area
* Paying attention to your surroundings

During this exercise and your first flights, keep the front of the drone facing the same direction throughout the entire course. Do not rotate the drone at each corner. Then repeat the course while rotating the drone at each corner. This will help you understand how orientation affects your control inputs.
