# Technical Memo

!!! abstract "Key Takeaways"
    - Turn-in point 4 of 4, due **Thu, Dec 10**. Worth **5%** of your course grade.
    - Write it as a contractor reporting to a project manager: everything they need, nothing they
      do not.
    - **No more than one page of writing.** Figures and tables can add more pages.
    - Number every figure, give it a caption, and refer to it in the text.

---

## Purpose

Imagine you were hired as a contractor to fly this mission. The memo is what you hand the project
manager afterwards. They were not there, so it has to tell them what you did, what you found, and how
far they can trust it.

---

## Required sections

Use these headings, in this order:

### Header

To, From (both partners), Date, and a title.

### Project scope and purpose

Who the client is, what the problem is, and what the data is for.

### Flight plan

The settings you chose and **why**: altitude, overlap, gimbal angle, pattern, and aircraft.

### Flight execution

How the mission went. What changed from the plan, and why.

### Data summary and results

The products you made and what they show. Your measurement or observation goes here, with figures.

### Limitations

What went wrong, and what the data cannot be used for. For example: trees modeled poorly, a gap in
coverage, a measurement that depends on a feature that was hard to see.

!!! tip "Check one number (encouraged, not required)"
    If you can, compare one drone measurement against something measured another way: a taped
    distance, a stall count, or a dimension from a plan. Report the difference in this section. It is
    the strongest evidence a client can get that your data is trustworthy. See
    [Measurement Fundamentals](../gen_reading/measurement_fundamentals.md).

### Conclusion

Your answer and your recommendation, in two or three sentences.

## Writing conventions

- Define every acronym on first use: UAS, GSD, AGL, DSM.
- Give units every time.
- Do not report more digits than your data supports. A parking stall is not known to the millimeter.
- Professional writing, grammar, and organization count.

## Grading

Scored on three criteria, 10 points each, **30 points total**. Both partners get the same score.

| Criterion | Exceeds (10) | Meets (8) | Below (0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Required sections** | All six sections present, each complete, and every flight setting explained | Most sections present; one is missing or thin, or settings listed without reasons | Most sections missing, or no memo |
| **Results and limitations** | Figures numbered, captioned, and referred to in the text; results answer the purpose; limitations stated honestly | Results shown, but figures not referenced, or limitations thin | Results missing or unsupported |
| **Professional writing** | One page of writing or less, clear organization, correct grammar and spelling | Readable, but over length or with several errors | Hard to follow or unprofessional |
