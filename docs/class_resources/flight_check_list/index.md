# Flight Checklists

!!! abstract "Key Takeaways"
    - A checklist turns a good pilot's memory into a repeatable procedure that anyone on the crew
      can follow.
    - Run the **pre-flight** list before you power anything on, and the **post-flight** list before
      you leave the site.
    - Most drone incidents come from skipped setup steps, not from difficult flying.
    - For engineering work, the checklist is also part of your record: it shows the data was
      collected deliberately and can be defended later.

---

## Why Checklists

Aviation adopted the checklist after crews found that experienced pilots still forgot steps under
time pressure. The same is true on a construction site. You will be flying with a client waiting,
weather changing, and a schedule to keep. A written list means the aircraft gets the same
preparation on a busy day as on a quiet one.

For an engineer there is a second reason. If a measurement is ever questioned, being able to show
that the equipment was checked, the site was surveyed, and the conditions were recorded is part of
what makes the data **defensible**.

---

## When to Use Each List

| List | When | What it protects |
|------|------|------------------|
| [Pre-Flight](pre_flight/pre_general.md) | Before powering on, at the site | The aircraft, the people nearby, and the flight |
| [Post-Flight](post_flight/post_general.md) | Before leaving the site | The data, the batteries, and the next crew |

Work through the list in order and do not skip ahead. If an item fails, stop and resolve it before
continuing. A checklist only works if "no" means the flight waits.

---

## Before You Fly

If you are new to the aircraft, read [Flight Basics](../../gen_reading/flight_basics.md) first. It covers
what each part does, what the controls do, and what the automated safety features will and will not
do for you.
