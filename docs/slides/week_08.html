GPR: soil sets the depth, not the sensor. Wet clay can cut it to under a metre; dry sand allows several. Drone GPR flies within about a metre of the ground; most GPR is still pushed on a cart. InSAR: a satellite product you download (USGS uses it for subsidence from groundwater pumping). It is the "radar" in most subsidence stories. Magnetometer: ferrous metal only. DOE's NETL switched from helicopters to drones around 2015 to find undocumented oil and gas wells. Gravimeter: drone systems err by about 0.5-3 mGal; a sinkhole void is about 0.01-0.13 mGal. Ground surveys can find voids by gravity; drones cannot yet. Methane laser: reads concentration times path length (ppm·m), not a point value; a leak rate needs wind and a model. Hyperspectral: 100+ narrow bands against multispectral's 4-10; gigabytes per flight. Water depth: green LiDAR reaches about twice the Secchi depth in clear water; tethered sonar works in muddy water. Air quality: finds the plume; weak for compliance numbers. Gamma: legacy uranium mines on the Colorado Plateau. Sources: .claude/local/scratch/week08_check/other_sensors.md in the course repo.
