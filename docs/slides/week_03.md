# Same drone, two flights. Same rules?

A friend flies it at the park for fun. You fly it to photograph a client's roof for a repair estimate.

<!--

---

<!-- _class: answer -->

## No — the purpose decides the rules

- **Recreational** flying: the exception in 49 U.S.C. 44809, with the **TRUST** certificate
- **Anything else** — a job, a client, a class mapping project, research: **Part 107**
- Part 107 needs a **remote pilot certificate** and an aircraft registered for Part 107
- In this class, a **TA with a Part 107 certificate** is remote pilot in command

## The operating limits (§107.51 and others)

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| Altitude | 400 ft above ground level (AGL), or within 400 ft of a structure |
| Speed | 87 knots (100 mph) |
| Visibility | 3 statute miles from the control station |
| Clouds | 500 ft below, 2,000 ft horizontally |
| Sight | Visual line of sight — yours or a visual observer's |
| Right of way | Yield to every other aircraft |
| Alcohol | Not within 8 hours, and under 0.04 % |

# A tower is 300 ft tall. Can you inspect its top at 650 ft AGL?

A) No, never above 400 ft · B) Yes, if you stay within 400 ft of the tower · C) Only with a waiver

<!--
Groups 1 min, then class.
Likely answer: A.
Follow-up: "How high could you go over a 1,000 ft tower?"
-->

---

<!-- _class: answer -->

## B — the structure exception

- Within **400 ft horizontally** of a structure, you may fly up to **400 ft above its top**
- 300 ft tower + 400 ft = **700 ft AGL** allowed, so 650 ft is fine
- Step away from the tower and the 400 ft AGL limit applies again
- An inspection flight is exactly what the exception is for

# Sunset was 20 minutes ago. Can you finish the flight?

A) No — Part 107 is daylight only · B) Yes, with anti-collision lighting · C) Only with a night waiver

<!--
Groups 1 min, then class.
Likely answer: A or C. Many study guides still say daylight only; the rule changed in 2021.
-->

---

<!-- _class: answer -->

## B — night flying is allowed

- Since **2021**, night operations need no waiver (§107.29)
- The pilot must have passed the current test or training
- **Anti-collision lights visible for 3 statute miles**, flashing enough to avoid a collision
- The same lights are required in **civil twilight** — 30 minutes before sunrise and after sunset

![w:900](../part_107_license/images/w05_fig11_airspace_classes.svg)

<p class="caption">Airspace classes, stacked. Controlled airspace needs permission before you fly.</p>

## Where you need permission

- **Controlled airspace** — Class B, C, D and **surface** Class E: ATC authorization (§107.41)
- **LAANC**: authorization in seconds, through an FAA-approved app, up to a set ceiling
- **FAA DroneZone**: everything LAANC can't do, which takes longer
- **Restricted areas**: permission from the controlling agency
- **Temporary flight restrictions (TFRs)**: check for them before every flight
- **BYU campus is inside Provo's Class D** — every campus flight needs LAANC *and* a BYU permit

# A home football game at LaVell Edwards Stadium.

A TA with a Part 107 certificate wants to film it from the air. Can they?

<!--
Groups 2 min, then class.
Likely answer: "Yes, with LAANC" — campus is Class D, so students who listened will reach for LAANC.
Follow-up: "What if the game ended two hours ago?"
-->

---

<!-- _class: answer -->

## No — a standing flight restriction covers the stadium

- FAA notice **FDC 0/0367**: no manned or unmanned aircraft within **3 NM** and **3,000 ft AGL** of a stadium with **30,000+ seats**
- Applies to NFL, MLB, **NCAA Division I football** and major races, **1 hour before to 1 hour after**
- LaVell Edwards Stadium seats about **62,000** — every home game qualifies
- LAANC cannot authorize it; only the event, security or the broadcaster can get a waiver
- Fines up to **$75,000** per violation, and drones can be seized

# Your 0.6 lb drone has no Declaration of Compliance.

Can you fly it over people at an outdoor graduation? A) Yes, it is light · B) No · C) Only if they agree

<!--
Groups 1.5 min, then class.
Likely answer: A.
This is the most-missed topic on the real test — about three in four people get it wrong.
-->

---

<!-- _class: answer -->

## B — over 0.55 lb needs a declared category

- **Category 1** is 0.55 lb or less, with no exposed parts that could cut skin
- Heavier aircraft need an FAA-accepted **Declaration of Compliance** for Category 2 or 3
- The **manufacturer** declares it; the **pilot** checks the label
- Category 3 may never fly over an open-air crowd; sustained flight over crowds needs Remote ID
- Moving vehicles have their own limits (§107.145)

## The paperwork

- **Register** every Part 107 aircraft, whatever it weighs, and mark the number on it
- **Remote ID**: three ways to comply
- **Carry** your certificate and photo ID; show them when asked (§107.7)
- **Preflight**: weather, airspace, NOTAMs and TFRs, aircraft condition, crew briefing

# The Remote ID warning comes on mid-flight.

What do you do? A) Keep flying, it's only a broadcast · B) Land as soon as practicable · C) Climb for a better signal

<!--
Groups 1 min, then class.
Likely answer: A.
-->

---

<!-- _class: answer -->

## B — land as soon as practicable

- A standard Remote ID aircraft that stops broadcasting must **land as soon as practicable** (§89.110)
- Without Remote ID the flight is not legal, unless you are inside an FAA-recognized area
- Most aircraft also refuse to take off with Remote ID failed

# You clip a parked car. The mirror costs $650 to fix.

Who do you tell, and how soon?

<!--
Groups 1.5 min, then class.
Likely answers: "nobody", "the police", or "the NTSB".
-->

---

<!-- _class: answer -->

## The FAA, within 10 days

- Report to the **FAA within 10 calendar days** (§107.9)
- Reportable: **serious injury**, **loss of consciousness**, or **property damage over $500** (repair or value)
- The aircraft itself does not count toward the $500
- Also exchange details with the car's owner, as with any accident

## Getting your certificate

1. Create an **IACRA** account and get your FAA Tracking Number (FTN)
2. Schedule the test with **PSI** — locally at UVU's testing centre at the Provo airport
3. Pass: 60 questions, 2 hours, **70 %**. *As of October 2026 the fee was about $175; check PSI when you book.*
4. Apply in IACRA with your exam ID; the **TSA** checks your background
5. Print the **temporary certificate**; the plastic card comes by mail

Week 9 is all about passing that test.

## When a flight goes wrong

- **Lost link**: the aircraft should return home on its own — know where "home" is set
- **Fly-away**: keep your eyes on it, warn people nearby, note where it goes
- **Low battery**: land early; cold days drain batteries faster
- **Battery fire or swelling**: don't fly it; move it outdoors onto a non-flammable surface
- Details: [Common Flight Issues](../gen_reading/flight_issues.md)

# Your aircraft stops responding and starts drifting away.

What are the first three things you do?

<!--
Groups 2 min, then class.
Likely answer: "Chase it." Running after a drone while looking up is how people get hurt.
-->

---

<!-- _class: answer -->

## Stay calm, keep sight, warn

1. **Keep your eyes on it** and tell your visual observer
2. **Trigger return-to-home**, or try to regain the link
3. **Warn people** where it is heading; note where it lands
- Afterwards: report it if it caused injury or more than $500 of damage
