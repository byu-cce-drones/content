Pairs, 1 minute. FE style: one correct answer and three built from common slips. Likely wrong answers: C (used 270° − Az, measuring from the wrong axis) and A (right angle, wrong quadrant).

h = H + N is where drone and GNSS work most often goes wrong, even though the FE treats it lightly. NGS is replacing NAD 83 and NAVD 88 with modernized reference frames; verify current status before relying on this beyond the exam.

Discussion, 2 minutes, before showing the bottom half of the slide if you can: "A drone survey's surface comes back about 17 m below the benchmark elevation printed on the plans. The drone was RTK. What went wrong?" Answer: nothing in the measurement — the drone's heights are ellipsoid heights (h) and the benchmark is an orthometric height (H). At BYU the geoid is 16.93 m below the ellipsoid. Follow-up: "Which way do you convert?" H = h − N; N is negative here, so H = h + 16.93 m. Have them say the sign out loud. On the FE: know which surface GNSS uses, which surface elevations use, and h = H + N with the sign of N given in the problem. Source for N: NGS GEOID18 web service at 40.2466 N, 111.6476 W (the Engineering Building), N = −16.93 m ± 0.05 m, checked 2026-11-17. The Bentley altitude-reference choice is section 2 of the Bentley iTwin guide.

The strip of western Utah west of 114° W is in UTM Zone 11. EPSG:32612 is WGS 84 / UTM zone 12N, the system the Bentley guide and QGIS page use for every class product.

Discussion, 2 minutes: "A crew stakes a property corner 1,000.00 ft from a monument. The distance came from state plane coordinates; they tape 1,000.00 ft. Is the corner too far or too short, and by how much?" Answer: too short. Ground = grid ÷ combined factor = 1,000.00 ÷ 0.99970504 = 1,000.295 ft, so the taped point is 0.295 ft (3½ in) short of where the coordinates put it. Follow-up: "Why is the factor smaller at BYU than in St. George?" Higher ground means a smaller elevation factor; the scale factor depends on where you are in the zone. Do not quote a St. George number; the point is the direction. On the FE: expect one multiplication or division with a given combined factor, or building the combined factor from a given scale factor and elevation (elevation factor about R / (R + h)). The trap is converting the wrong way. Sources, all for the Engineering Building (40.2466 N, 111.6476 W), checked 2026-11-17: USGS EPQS ground elevation 1,416.82 m; GEOID18 N = −16.93 m, so h = 1,399.89 m; NGS NCAT with that h: Utah Central scale factor 0.99992458, combined 0.99970504; UTM 12 scale 0.99963734, combined 0.99941786. Elevation factor 0.99978 is combined ÷ scale and matches for both.

Week 5 link: repeating a measurement, or closing a loop on yourself, tests precision. A traverse with a stretched tape closes perfectly and is still wrong by the stretch. A traverse that fails tolerance gets re-measured, not adjusted.
