# Part 107 Knowledge Review

This is the Week 9 reading: concise study notes for the FAA Part 107 knowledge test, organized the way the test is. The rules themselves are taught in [The Part 107 Exam](faa_exam_planning_and_overview.md) (Week 3); this page assumes you have read it and concentrates on what the test asks and how it asks it. Links to free study material are on the [Study Resources](part_107_resources.md) page.

!!! abstract "Key Takeaways"
    - **The test:** 60 scored questions (plus 5 unscored), 120 minutes, 70 % to pass, at a PSI testing center. Regulations are 48 % of it, Operations 25 %, Airspace 20 %, Weather 5 %, Loading 2 %.
    - **Regulatory limits:** 400 ft AGL, 3 SM visibility, 500 ft below and 2,000 ft horizontal from clouds, keep the aircraft in sight, yield to every aircraft, lighting at night.
    - **Airspace:** ATC authorization in Class B, C, D and **surface** Class E only (§107.41). Everything else on a chart is MSL; your limits are AGL.
    - **The most-missed question** on the real test is the Declaration of Compliance for flying over people. Know who files it and what it covers.
    - **Figures:** most chart and weather questions are answered by finding the area number on the figure and reading one symbol with the legend.

## Exam Day

- **Bring:** a government photo ID showing your date of birth, signature and physical address (no P.O. box); your FAA Tracking Number; the failed score report if this is a retake.
- **Leave in the car:** phone, smart watch, notes, books. Nothing electronic goes in with you except a calculator with its memory cleared in front of the proctor, and the test software has its own calculator anyway.
- **Provided:** scratch paper, a pen, and the testing-supplement figures — a printed FAA-CT-8080-2H or the figure on screen inside the question.
- **Pace:** two minutes a question. Answer the ones you know, flag the rest, come back. There is no penalty for guessing, so never leave a blank.
- **Afterwards:** the score report lists the ACS codes of every question you missed. Keep it; it is what you study if you have to retake, and you need its Exam ID for the IACRA application.

## How to Read a Figure Question

A figure question reads like this:

> **Refer to FAA-CT-8080-2H, Figure 22, area 2.** What is the CTAF at Coeur d'Alene?

1. **Find the figure.** Figures are numbered in the supplement's table of contents; the sectional chart excerpts start at Figure 20, the METAR/TAF page is Figure 12, and the chart legend is at the front.
2. **Find the area number.** Each chart excerpt has large red circled numbers. Go to that number, then look near it for the airport or feature named in the question.
3. **Use the legend.** Do not guess what a symbol means. The legend page shows every airport symbol, airspace line, frequency box and obstacle marker.
4. **Apply one rule.** Nearly every figure question ends with a single regulation or definition: does this airspace need authorization, what is the floor of this shelf, which frequency is the CTAF, which direction is the aircraft.
5. **Check the units.** Chart altitudes are MSL; obstacle heights in parentheses are AGL; the question will often offer both as answer choices.

For the example above: in area 2, find Coeur d'Alene. In the airport data block the frequency followed by a white "C" in a magenta circle is the Common Traffic Advisory Frequency, **122.8**.

Practice this with the local copy of the supplement linked from the Week 3 reading — on a screen, because the FAA now embeds the figures in the question rather than relying on the printed book.

## Regulations (48 %) — Key Points

Learn these with their section numbers; the test sometimes quotes them.

- **Who may fly (§107.12):** the remote pilot in command (PIC) holds the certificate and is current; anyone else may fly the controls only under the PIC's direct supervision.
- **Carry it (§107.7):** certificate and ID in your possession; show them to the FAA, NTSB, TSA or any law enforcement officer who asks.
- **Operational limits (§107.51):** 400 ft AGL unless within 400 ft of a structure (then 400 ft above its top); 87 kt / 100 mph; 3 SM visibility from the control station; 500 ft below and 2,000 ft horizontal from clouds.
- **See it (§107.31, §107.33):** visual line of sight with unaided eyes (glasses allowed) by the PIC, the person flying, or a visual observer who can communicate with them.
- **Night and twilight (§107.29):** both are allowed with anti-collision lighting visible 3 SM; night also needs a test or training dated after April 6, 2021. Civil twilight is 30 minutes before sunrise and 30 minutes after sunset (outside Alaska).
- **Over people (§107.39, Subpart D):** only participants, people under cover or in stopped vehicles — or within Category 1, 2, 3 or 4. Categories 2 and 3 exist only through a **Declaration of Compliance** filed by the manufacturer; Category 3 never over open-air assemblies. Over moving vehicles (§107.145): closed site with notice, or transit only.
- **Right of way (§107.37):** yield to every aircraft, manned or not.
- **Airspace (§107.41):** prior ATC authorization in Class B, C, D and the surface area of Class E; through LAANC or DroneZone.
- **Airports and restricted areas (§107.43, §107.45):** no interference with traffic patterns; permission from the controlling agency for prohibited and restricted areas. **TFRs (§107.47):** obey them.
- **Alcohol and drugs (§107.27):** 8 hours bottle to throttle; 0.04 alcohol concentration; no impairing drugs.
- **Emergencies (§107.21):** deviate as needed; report to the FAA *on request*.
- **Safety events (§107.9):** report to the FAA within **10 calendar days** for serious injury, loss of consciousness, or over $500 of damage to someone else's property.
- **Registration (§107.13, §48.15):** every Part 107 aircraft, regardless of weight; the 0.55 lb exemption is recreational only.
- **Remote ID (Part 89):** standard aircraft, broadcast module (takeoff location, keep it in sight), or a FRIA. Broadcast fails → land as soon as practicable.
- **Certificate (§107.61–107.65):** 16 or older, English, initial test, then free recurrent training every 24 calendar months; the certificate never expires.
- **Waivers (§107.205):** VLOS, visual observer, over people, moving vehicles, altitude/speed/visibility/clouds, multiple aircraft, from a moving vehicle, night *lighting*, controlled airspace. Not night itself, not alcohol, not registration, not reckless operation.

## Airspace (20 %)

- **Class A:** 18,000 ft MSL and up — not for Part 107.
- **Class B:** the busiest airports; solid blue lines; shelves labeled ceiling/floor in hundreds of feet **MSL** ("100/40"). Authorization required.
- **Class C:** medium airports; solid magenta; core plus shelf, same label style. Authorization required.
- **Class D:** towered airports; dashed blue; ceiling in brackets. Authorization required.
- **Class E:** controlled airspace that is none of the above. Its **floor** is the key: the surface (dashed magenta — authorization required), 700 ft AGL (shaded magenta edge), or 1,200 ft AGL (shaded blue edge, or by default). Below a 700 or 1,200 ft floor a drone is in Class G and needs no authorization.
- **Class G:** uncontrolled. No authorization, every other rule applies.
- **Special-use airspace:** **Prohibited** (P-) — never. **Restricted** (R-) — permission from the controlling agency. **MOA** and **military training routes** (IR/VR; a four-digit number means at or below 1,500 ft AGL) — not forbidden, but a serious hazard; check the chart margin for times of use. **Warning** and **Alert** areas — caution.
- **TFRs:** by NOTAM; mandatory; check before every flight. Stadiums, wildfires, VIP movements, launches.

Quick rule: authorization for **B, C, D and surface E**; G is open; everything military needs a look at the chart margin.

## Sectional Charts — What to Read Quickly

- **Airport symbols:** blue = towered, magenta = non-towered; the data block gives the elevation, runway length, lighting and frequencies.
- **Frequencies:** the number before a circled **C** is the CTAF; a star means part-time tower; the Chart Supplement has the rest.
- **Airspace lines:** solid blue (B), solid magenta (C), dashed blue (D), dashed magenta (surface E), shaded magenta (E at 700 ft), shaded blue (E at 1,200 ft).
- **Obstacles:** tower symbol with the top in MSL, and the height AGL in parentheses underneath. Lightning-bolt marks mean the obstruction is lit. A group obstacle symbol means several.
- **Maximum elevation figures:** the big numbers in each grid square are the highest terrain or obstacle in that square, in thousands and hundreds of feet MSL.
- **Latitude and longitude:** tick marks along the chart edges and the grid lines; some questions give a coordinate and ask you to name the airport there.
- **Runway direction:** the runway diagram inside an airport symbol shows its orientation; the runway number is its magnetic heading ÷ 10.

## Weather (5 %)

- **METAR:** current observation. Order: station, day/time Z, wind (true direction, knots, G for gusts), visibility SM, present weather (`-RA` light rain, `RA` moderate, `+RA` heavy, `BR` mist, `FG` fog, `TS` thunderstorm), sky (`FEW`, `SCT`, `BKN`, `OVC` plus base in hundreds of feet AGL), temperature/dew point °C, altimeter inHg, remarks.
- **Ceiling** is the lowest **BKN** or **OVC** layer (or vertical visibility), never FEW or SCT.
- **TAF:** forecast for an airport, 24 to 30 hours, with `FM`, `TEMPO`, `BECMG` and `PROB` change groups.
- **AIRMET / SIGMET / Convective SIGMET / PIREP:** advisories for moderate, severe, thunderstorm, and pilot-reported conditions.
- **Theory:** stable air = smooth, poor visibility, steady rain, fog; unstable air = turbulence, good visibility, showers and thunderstorms. Temperature near dew point = fog or low cloud. Standard lapse rate 2 °C per 1,000 ft.
- **Density altitude:** high, hot, humid = thin air = less thrust, shorter flights.
- **Tools:** AviationWeather.gov; 1-800-WX-BRIEF or 1800wxbrief.com for a briefing and NOTAMs.

## Loading and Performance (2 %, minor)

- Follow the loading instructions in the aircraft's **flight manual** to keep the CG in limits.
- **Load factor** in a level turn: about 1.15 at 30°, 1.41 at 45°, 2.0 at 60°. A 33 lb aircraft at 30° of bank loads the structure with about 38 lb.
- The remote PIC, not the manufacturer, is responsible for determining that the aircraft can perform the flight as loaded.

## Operations (25 %)

### Radio and airports

- Standard phraseology, phonetic alphabet (Alpha, Bravo, Charlie, …), concise calls.
- **CTAF/UNICOM:** pilots self-announce position at non-towered airports; listen to it to know where traffic is.
- **Traffic pattern:** upwind, crosswind, downwind, base, final. Standard patterns turn **left**, so "left downwind" has the runway on the pilot's left. Runway 13 is flown on about 130°; its left downwind runs 310° on the northeast side.
- **Chart Supplement:** the most complete source of airport information (frequencies, pattern altitude, remarks).
- **Light gun signals** from a tower: steady green (cleared), steady red (stop/give way), flashing white (return to start), alternating red/green (extreme caution).
- Obtain ATC authorization through LAANC or DroneZone where required and follow any altitude or time limits it carries.

### Emergencies

- **Lost link:** know the aircraft's programmed behaviour (return-to-home, hover, land) and set the home point and return altitude before takeoff.
- **Fly-away:** keep watching it, try return-to-home, warn people, note where it went. Report under §107.9 if it hurts someone or damages property.
- **Lithium batteries:** swelling, heat or smoke means do not fly; isolate the pack outdoors on a non-flammable surface. Store partly charged, at room temperature.
- **GPS loss, interference, frequency conflicts:** fly manually or land; keep the aircraft in sight.
- Practical detail in [Common Flight Issues](../gen_reading/flight_issues.md).

### Decision-making and physiology

- **IMSAFE:** Illness, Medication, Stress, Alcohol, Fatigue, Emotion/Eating.
- **PAVE:** Pilot, Aircraft, enVironment, External pressures.
- **Hazardous attitudes:** anti-authority, impulsivity, invulnerability, macho, resignation — and their antidotes (Week 3 reading, Figure 24). The test gives a quote and asks which attitude it is.
- **CRM:** brief the crew, assign roles (visual observer, telemetry), debrief.
- **Physiology:** dehydration and heat stroke on summer sites; hyperventilation (slow the breathing); fatigue is "an impaired state"; night vision takes about 30 minutes to adapt and is lost to a bright light; even small amounts of alcohol impair judgment.

### Maintenance and inspection

- Follow the manufacturer's schedule; inspect before every flight (props, frame, battery, link, control surfaces); keep records of maintenance and firmware.

## Common Traps

The wrong answer offered on the test is usually one of these.

1. **MSL vs AGL.** Everything printed on a sectional is MSL except obstacle heights in parentheses. Cloud bases in a METAR and the 400 ft rule are AGL.
2. **Obstacle heights.** "Top 1,549 (1,049)" means 1,549 ft MSL, 1,049 ft above the ground. The question will offer both.
3. **Ceiling** = lowest BKN or OVC layer only. SCT at 1,500 ft is not a ceiling.
4. **Zulu time.** `121856Z` is the 12th at 18:56 UTC. Utah is UTC−7 in winter (MST) and UTC−6 in summer (MDT), so 18:56 Z is 11:56 am MST or 12:56 pm MDT. The supplement's time-conversion table is Figure 27.
5. **Wind direction.** METAR and TAF winds are **true**; tower and ATIS winds are magnetic.
6. **Class E floors.** Only the **surface** Class E area needs authorization. The 700 and 1,200 ft shaded edges are above you.
7. **Restricted vs MOA vs TFR vs prohibited.** Restricted: permission from the controlling agency. MOA and military routes: allowed but hazardous, check the times. TFR: a NOTAM, mandatory. Prohibited: never.
8. **Who gets the report.** Emergency deviations: the FAA, *on request*. Safety events: the FAA, within 10 days. The NTSB and ATC are the wrong answers.
9. **Night is not a waiver item.** It needs 3 SM lighting and a post-2021 test or training. Civil twilight needs the lighting only.
10. **Registration weight.** Every Part 107 aircraft is registered; 0.55 lb is the *recreational* threshold.
11. **Declaration of Compliance.** The **manufacturer** files it; it is what makes an aircraft Category 2 or 3; the pilot checks the label. Category 1 needs none; Category 4 uses an airworthiness certificate instead.
12. **Remote ID module vs standard.** Module broadcasts the takeoff location and must stay in sight; standard broadcasts the control station. Either way, broadcast fails → land as soon as practicable.
13. **Load factor.** 30° → 1.15, 45° → 1.41, 60° → 2.0. Multiply the weight.
14. **Traffic pattern geometry.** Runway number × 10 = heading. Left traffic puts the runway on the pilot's left; draw it before you answer.
15. **"Monitoring the frequency" is not authorization.** Class B, C, D and surface E need *prior* ATC authorization, obtained before launch.

## Short Practice Questions

1. What is the maximum altitude allowed for sUAS operations without a waiver?
2. Which airspace classes require ATC authorization before a Part 107 flight?
3. What does a METAR report provide?
4. What mnemonic helps assess personal fitness to fly?
5. How far horizontally must you remain from clouds during Part 107 operations?
6. A METAR reads `SCT015 BKN030`. What is the ceiling?
7. Who files the Declaration of Compliance that makes an aircraft Category 2?

??? note "Answers"
    1. 400 ft AGL (or up to 400 ft above a structure while within 400 ft of it).
    2. Class B, C, D, and the surface area of Class E designated for an airport (§107.41). Not Class E with a 700 or 1,200 ft floor, and not Class G.
    3. The current surface weather observation at an airport: wind, visibility, present weather, clouds, temperature/dew point, altimeter, remarks.
    4. IMSAFE.
    5. 2,000 ft horizontally (and 500 ft below).
    6. 3,000 ft AGL — the lowest broken or overcast layer. The scattered layer at 1,500 ft is not a ceiling.
    7. The manufacturer. The remote pilot checks that the aircraft is listed on an FAA-accepted DoC and carries the category label.

## Two-Week Study Plan

Test day is day 14. Two short sessions a day beat one long one.

| Days | Do |
|---|---|
| **1–2** | Reread [The Part 107 Exam](faa_exam_planning_and_overview.md). Make a one-page sheet of the numbers: 400, 3, 500/2,000, 87/100, 8 h, 0.04, 10 days, $500, 0.55 lb, 24 months, 14 days. Read AC 107-2A chapters on operating rules and over-people categories. |
| **3–5** | Airspace. Open the supplement on a screen and work every chart excerpt: name the airspace at each red number, the floor of every shelf, every CTAF. Read PHAK chapter 15 (airspace) and 14 (airports). |
| **6–7** | Weather and loading. Decode ten real METARs on AviationWeather.gov. PHAK chapters 12–13 (weather) and the density altitude and load factor pages of chapters 5 and 11. |
| **8–9** | Operations. PHAK chapter 2 (decision-making) and 17 (aeromedical); AC 107-2A on emergencies and batteries. Learn the five attitudes with their antidotes. |
| **10–11** | Practice tests. The FAA sample questions (no key; answers are on the Week 9 slides), PSI's free UAG sample test, and one of the study-guide banks. Log every miss by topic. |
| **12–13** | Retest your weak topics only. Reread the Common Traps list above. Confirm your ID, FTN and appointment time. |
| **14** | Test. Arrive early, read every question twice, flag and return. |
