# Part 107 Study Resources

A curated set of study resources for the FAA Part 107 knowledge test, organized by type, with a note on what each is good for. Everything from the FAA is free and public domain. Links were checked October 2026.

!!! abstract "Key Takeaways"
    - **Start with the FAA's own material:** AC 107-2A for the rules, the testing supplement for the figures, the Pilot's Handbook chapters for airspace, weather and decision-making.
    - **Check the date.** Several FAA study aids predate the 2021 rule changes (night, operations over people, Remote ID) and the 2025 test weighting; use them for airspace, weather and charts, and get the rules from AC 107-2A and the Week 3 reading.
    - **Practice on a screen** with real chart figures, then take timed practice tests and log every miss by topic.

---

## Official FAA Resources — Study

- [AC 107-2A, Small Unmanned Aircraft Systems](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_107-2A.pdf){target='_blank'} (PDF, 2021) — The FAA's plain-language guide to Part 107, current on night operations, operations over people and Remote ID. The single best rules reference.
- [Airman Knowledge Testing Supplement, FAA-CT-8080-2H](https://www.faa.gov/training_testing/testing/supplements){target='_blank'} — The figures the test uses. A reduced-size local copy is linked at the top of [The Part 107 Exam](faa_exam_planning_and_overview.md). Practise finding figures on a screen.
- [Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge, FAA-H-8083-25C](https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/phak){target='_blank'} — Written for manned pilots, but the test draws on it: chapter 2 (decision-making), 5 (load factor), 10 (weight and balance), 11 (density altitude), 12–13 (weather), 14 (airports), 15 (airspace), 17 (aeromedical).
- [Remote Pilot – Small UAS Study Guide, FAA-G-8082-22](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/uas/resources/policy_library/remote_pilot_study_guide.pdf){target='_blank'} (PDF, 2016) — The FAA's original study guide. Still good for airspace, charts and weather; it **predates the 2021 rules**, so take night, over-people and Remote ID from AC 107-2A instead.
- [FAA UAG sample questions](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/training_testing/testing/test_questions/uag_questions.pdf){target='_blank'} (PDF, 2021) — 46 questions in the real test's wording, referencing the supplement figures. **No answer key**; the Week 9 slides work through the ones the class uses.
- [PSI UAG Applicant Information Bulletin](https://media.psiexams.com/faa/UAG_Information_Bulletin.pdf){target='_blank'} (PDF) — Test rules, ID requirements, what you may bring, and the current topic weighting (effective 2025-09-29).
- [PSI free UAG sample test](https://faa.psiexams.com/){target='_blank'} — Log in, then Exam Resources → UAG. The closest thing to the live test's format, with figures embedded on screen.
- [Become a Certificated Remote Pilot](https://www.faa.gov/uas/commercial_operators/become_a_drone_pilot/){target='_blank'} — The FAA's six-step page: FTN, scheduling, the test, IACRA, TSA, temporary certificate.
- [Part 107 Recurrent Training, ALC-677](https://www.faasafety.gov/gslac/ALC/course_content.aspx?pf=1&preview=true&cID=677){target='_blank'} — The free online course that keeps a certificate current, every 24 calendar months.

## Official FAA Resources — Flying

- [14 CFR Part 107](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-F/part-107){target='_blank'} and [Part 89, Remote ID](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-F/part-89){target='_blank'} — The regulations, always current, on eCFR.
- [FAA Certificated Remote Pilots](https://www.faa.gov/uas/commercial_operators){target='_blank'} — The FAA's Part 107 hub: operations over people, waivers, airspace authorization.
- [LAANC](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/laanc){target='_blank'} — How near-real-time airspace authorization works, and the current list of approved providers.
- [FAA DroneZone](https://faadronezone-access.faa.gov/){target='_blank'} — Registration, manual authorizations, waivers, safety event reports.
- [Remote ID](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/remote_id){target='_blank'} — The three ways to comply and the list of accepted modules and aircraft.
- [AviationWeather.gov](https://www.aviationweather.gov/){target='_blank'} — METARs, TAFs and graphical forecasts; decode a few every day while you study.
- [Temporary Flight Restrictions](https://tfr.faa.gov/){target='_blank'} — The live TFR list; check it before every outdoor flight.

!!! note "Videos and the 2025 weighting"
    Every video above was recorded before the September 2025 change that made Regulations 48 % of the test and cut Weather to 5 %. Their content is still right; give the regulations more of your time than they do.

## Recommended Study Strategy

The day-by-day version is the [two-week plan](part_107_knowledge_review.md#two-week-study-plan) on the Knowledge Review page.

1. **Rules first:** reread [The Part 107 Exam](faa_exam_planning_and_overview.md) and AC 107-2A; regulations are nearly half the test.
2. **Map the rest:** watch the [Rapid Slide Overview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GO1CXPsCKoQ), then the [Matt Kendall Full Review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zB9qzXaQ72s) or one of the other full courses, noting timestamps for anything confusing.
3. **Charts on a screen:** work every chart excerpt in the supplement with the legend open beside it.
4. **Targeted drills:** use the topic playlists and the PHAK chapters for weak areas.
5. **Simulated testing:** alternate the FAA sample questions, the PSI sample test, and the Rupprecht and King Schools banks; log every miss by topic.
6. **Final review:** the [Knowledge Review](part_107_knowledge_review.md) Common Traps list and mnemonics the night before.
