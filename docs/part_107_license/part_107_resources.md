# Part 107 Study Resources

Everything you need to pass the FAA Part 107 knowledge test is free; this page says what to read first, where each item is, and which ones repeat each other. Every link was checked in October 2026.

!!! abstract "Key Takeaways"
    - **Follow the six steps under Start here.** Each names the exact document, page or video to open; the ranked list after them says how long each takes (about 19 hours in all).
    - **Rules from AC 107-2A, figures from the testing supplement, airspace and weather from the Pilot's Handbook.** Anything dated before 2021 is wrong on night flying, operations over people and Remote ID; anything before September 2025 under-weights the regulations.
    - **Practise on a screen, then under time.** Work the chart figures with the legend open beside them, then take timed tests and log every miss by test area.

---

## Start here: how to study

Six steps, in order. The day-by-day version is the [two-week plan](part_107_knowledge_review.md#two-week-study-plan) on the Knowledge Review page, and the time each step takes is in the [ranked list](#the-top-8-ranked) below.

1. **Rules first — regulations are 48 % of the test.** Reread [The Part 107 Exam](faa_exam_planning_and_overview.md) and the [Knowledge Review](part_107_knowledge_review.md), then read chapters 5, 6 and 8 of [AC 107-2A](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_107-2A.pdf){target='_blank'}: the operating rules, the remote pilot certificate, and operations over people with the Declaration of Compliance.
2. **Charts on a screen — airspace is 20 % and the most-missed area.** Open the course copy of the testing supplement, [FAA-CT-8080-2H (PDF)](images/sport_rec_private_akts_FAA-CT-8080-2H_small.pdf), and keep the sectional chart legend (PDF page 13) open beside it in a second window. Work all sixteen sectional excerpts: Figures 20–26 (pages 53–59), 59 (page 90), 69–71 (pages 100–102), 74–76 (pages 105–107), 78 (page 109) and 80 (page 111). At every red number, name the airspace, the floor of any shelf, and the CTAF. The method is in [How to Read a Figure Question](part_107_knowledge_review.md#how-to-read-a-figure-question).
3. **Map the rest with one video.** Watch [Matt Kendall's full review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zB9qzXaQ72s){target='_blank'} (2024, 1 h 37 min) with the weighting table from the Knowledge Review beside you, and note the timestamp of anything that does not stick. The other full reviews cover the same ground; one is enough.
4. **Drill weak areas.** Use the [Drill by topic](#drill-by-topic) table below. It gives one source per test area: a chapter of AC 107-2A, a chapter of the Pilot's Handbook, a single Mr. Mig video, or a supplement figure.
5. **Test under time.** Work the [FAA sample questions](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/training_testing/testing/test_questions/uag_questions.pdf){target='_blank'} alongside the [Week 9 slides](../slides/week_09.html), take the [PSI sample test](https://faa.psiexams.com/){target='_blank'} (Exam Resources → Unmanned Aircraft Systems → UAG), read the explanations for your misses in [Rupprecht's 72 questions](https://jrupprechtlaw.com/part-107-knowledge-test/){target='_blank'}, then take the course [mini](mini_exam.md) and [60-question](60_question_exam.md) practice exams at two minutes a question. Log every miss by test area.
6. **Final review.** The [Common Traps](part_107_knowledge_review.md#common-traps) list and the mnemonics on the Knowledge Review the night before. Confirm your ID, FAA Tracking Number (FTN) and appointment time.

## All resources

Every link on this page, one line each. What each is and which overlap is explained in the [next section](#what-each-one-is-and-what-overlaps).

### Official FAA — study

- [AC 107-2A, Small Unmanned Aircraft Systems](https://www.faa.gov/documentlibrary/media/Advisory_Circular/AC_107-2A.pdf){target='_blank'} (PDF, 2021) — the rules, current.
- [Airman Knowledge Testing Supplement, FAA-CT-8080-2H](https://www.faa.gov/training_testing/testing/supplements){target='_blank'} — the test figures; [course copy (PDF)](images/sport_rec_private_akts_FAA-CT-8080-2H_small.pdf), reduced size.
- [Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge, FAA-H-8083-25C](https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/phak){target='_blank'} — airspace, weather, decision-making chapters.
- [Remote Pilot – Small UAS Study Guide, FAA-G-8082-22](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/uas/resources/policy_library/remote_pilot_study_guide.pdf){target='_blank'} (PDF, 2016) — pre-2021; airspace and weather only.
- [PSI UAG Applicant Information Bulletin](https://media.psiexams.com/faa/UAG_Information_Bulletin.pdf){target='_blank'} (PDF, 2025) — test-day rules and the current weighting.
- [Become a Certificated Remote Pilot](https://www.faa.gov/uas/commercial_operators/become_a_drone_pilot/){target='_blank'} — the FAA's six steps to the certificate.
- [Part 107 Recurrent Training, ALC-677](https://www.faasafety.gov/gslac/ALC/course_content.aspx?pf=1&preview=true&cID=677){target='_blank'} — free; keeps the certificate current every 24 months.

## What each one is, and what overlaps

| Resource | What it is | Date | Overlap or caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| AC 107-2A | The FAA's 104-page plain-language guide to Part 107. Chapters 4–8 follow the rule's subparts (registration, operating rules, certificate, maintenance, over people); the appendices hold risk-assessment tools and a checklist | Feb 2021 | The rules spine. Covers night, over people and Remote ID; still the active circular, no newer edition exists |
| Testing supplement FAA-CT-8080-2H | The figure book the test quotes. The official PDF is 176 MB; the course copy is 113 pages at reduced size | 2018, still current | Nothing else has the real figures. Sectional legend on page 13; pages 14–31 are Chart Supplement legends |
| Pilot's Handbook (PHAK) | Seventeen chapters for manned pilots, each on its own web page. The test draws on chapters 2, 5, 10–15 and 17 | 2023 | Airspace, weather and decision-making; not rule-dependent, so the date does not matter |
| FAA-G-8082-22 study guide | 88 pages; chapters 2–12 are condensed airspace, weather, decision-making and airport material | Aug 2016 | Duplicates the PHAK chapters above. Its regulations chapter is one sentence pointing to a cancelled circular; take rules from AC 107-2A |
| PSI bulletin | Six pages: what to bring, what is allowed in the room, and the weighting | Sep 2025 | The only source of the current weighting (Regulations 48 %, Operations 25 %, Airspace 20 %, Weather 5 %, Loading 2 %) |
| FAA UAG sample questions | 46 questions with ACS codes, in the test's wording, referencing supplement figures. No key | Aug 2021 | Rupprecht's 72 questions are in effect the key with explanations for this set; the Week 9 slides work the ones the class uses |
| PSI free sample test | PSI's own 46-question sample inside the testing software's format, figures on screen | Current | Same kind of questions as the FAA PDF, in the format you will see on test day |
| Rupprecht 72 questions | FAA-style questions in ACS order, each with its answer and explanation; cites Figures 12, 20–26, 59, 75 and 78 | Oct 2026 | The best free practice bank; covers night lighting, over-people categories and Remote ID |
| Rupprecht study guide | Mostly a reading list (CFR parts, circulars, PHAK pages) plus test-taking tips; promotes paid courses | Oct 2026 | Does the same job as our Knowledge Review and two-week plan, without explaining over people or Remote ID |
| King Schools | Up to 60 questions across six categories, timed at two minutes each | Live bank | Good for extra timed sets; the explanations beyond the first are in the paid course |
| Matt Kendall | Full-length review, 1 h 37 min | Jun 2024 | Post-2021 rules; recorded before the 2025 weighting, so give regulations more time than it does |
| Katia's Buzz Media | Slide-based review, 52 min | May 2021 | Same ground as Kendall in half the time; recorded just after the 2021 rules |
| Mr. Mig's Classroom | 19 videos, one topic each: latitude and longitude, traffic patterns, sectional questions, METAR and TAF, load factor, weather, density altitude, airspace classes, over people | 2021–2025 | The drill source in the table above |
| MikeSytes | 31 videos: a 12-part series (charts, weather, METAR, TAF, airports, load factor, crew resource management), question walk-throughs, and off-exam items (Part 108, enforcement) | Updated 2026 | Deep and current; skip the off-exam videos |
| Tony Northrup; Gary Glenn | Full-length reviews, 1 h 44 min and 1 h 53 min | 2017; 2018 | Pre-2021: both say night needs a waiver, flying over people is banned outright, there is no Remote ID, and the certificate is renewed by a test. All four are now wrong |

!!! note "Dates to watch for"
    **Before April 2021:** night flying required a waiver; operations over people were prohibited without one; Remote ID did not exist; recurrency was a paid test rather than free online training. Any study aid from before then teaches those old rules. **Before September 2025:** the weighting was different and the regulations were a smaller share of the test, so older guides and every video above spend less time on the rules than you should. **The four full-length video reviews** (Kendall, Katia's Buzz Media, Northrup, Glenn) cover the same material; watch one, and make it Kendall.
